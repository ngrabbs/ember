# Recovery latch correction and MOSFET survival checkpoint

2026-10-23. **The nuisance latch is corrected in the development schematic and stated simulations. MOSFET electrical/thermal screening is complete for the recorded fixtures; guaranteed hot SOA and physical survival remain unqualified.** This is the current correction to the [protection selection](rev_b_5v_recovery_protection_selection.md), superseding its original timer/gate values and 81-part native export. Existing shared interfaces and the PCB are unchanged.

## Why the earlier circuit failed

The low-FB sensitivity can clamp during load-release overshoot. At very low remaining load, the protected bank then takes milliseconds to discharge, exhausting the original 220 pF timer. Increasing that timer to 10 nF without wider checks was insufficient in cold/high-rate sensitivities. A 47 nF candidate entered the fault-warning region in the cold minimum-bank case. Reducing the timer to 10 nF caused a high-capacitance startup failure; retain 100 nF. A longer no-load hold also exposed persistent timer charging at the original divider threshold. R107/R108 now set 5.270 V nominal, separating the healthy regulator maximum from that cold clamp sensitivity. A 5.30 V candidate approached 5.5 V too closely in the high-trip slow fault and was rejected in favor of the intermediate ratio. Long no-load holds must show that the timer actually clears; a recovered output alone is insufficient.

An initial ideal regulator fixture also compensated the converter's 20 mΩ shunt instantaneously, introducing artificial high-frequency negative damping. The current fixture limits that correction to 1 kHz. This is a fixture correction, **not** a validated TPS552872 control-loop model. A tested RC damper did not resolve the release timing and is not installed. Reducing gate slew from 68 to 10 nF improves recovery, while the finite-current startup cases still pass.

The independent hard **input** short is different from loss of solar input or an **output** short. With no L2 and typical package inductances included, it produces 19.53 V internal pass VGS and 35.44 V protected output, violating 16 V gate / provisional 35 V output limits. The external gate-to-package-source clamp alone does not prevent the die-level source-inductance excursion. L2 reduces the tested internal pass VGS to 10.47 V, reverse-device peak current from 47.68 to 5.50 A, and protected peak to 32.05 V. The failed unguarded circuit is retained as a rejected counterexample.

## Current numerical evidence

[Fixtures, scripts, numerical evidence and native review](../verification/protection/latch/README.md) record **22 rail cases and 16 source cases**. Eight source cases are comparisons without L2; eight implement the installed source topology. The unguarded hard input short is expected to fail two numerical checks. Expected failure is not circuit acceptance.

- Cold/hot single load-release cases avoid latch-off; maximum timer voltage stays below 1.22 V, and the long-hold cases require final timer voltage below 0.1 V against the cited minimum warning threshold. Their protected peaks stay below the provisional 5.5 V ceiling.
- Finite-current cold/hot startup, high protected capacitance, shunt drift, deliberate reset, output short, input loss, finite-reservoir and shunt-bypass faults, added gate charge/weak gate-pull sensitivity and one-device-per-function cases are recorded.
- The new source high-reference 60 V fault peaks at about 34.72 V. With slower effective timer and extra gate charge, pass energy is about 39.19 mJ; the historical 100 nF source timer case was about 120 mJ. These are sensitivity fixtures, not a perfectly isolated timer-only comparison.
- The new cold/hot high-trip slow-overvoltage cases reach approximately **5.467 V**, below the provisional 5.5 V ceiling with only about 33 mV of modeled margin. Bridge cases peak around 5.221 V. Neither result expands the low-VCC accuracy guarantee or accepts arbitrary input/layout corners.
- The rail slow timer/bridge cases reach about 93 mJ total pass-bank post-event energy. Reported energies integrate from the event through each case stop; reset-case totals also include later healthy operation and must not be interpreted as a single isolated fault pulse. A true latch followed by an early UV reset remains off during cooldown and subsequently recovers with light startup load. The reset fixture holds a real fault longer than the earlier short transient, so it verifies an actual latch.

The scripts use equivalent timing capacitance to inject fast/slow timer-rate sensitivity: nominal 100 nF rail or 10 nF source, capacitor tolerance/TCR and 1.5× faster / 0.6× slower charge-rate assumptions. These are **sensitivity envelopes**, not a guarantee for every intermediate VCC or timer discharge/threshold corner. The LTC4364 table is primarily specified at 12 V; FB accuracy at 5 V remains outside the cited guaranteed row.

## Load admission and reset contract

Abrupt load transitions remain unqualified: the cold minimum-bank single release briefly reaches approximately 4.75 V, and repeated 0↔2 A changes every 5 ms cause deeper dips below 4.75 V. The revised circuit avoids latch-off in these cases. This separate undervoltage check remains an **expected rejection**. Do not silently call it a fully accepted 2 A transient rail.

The cold/hot controlled-load cases use **5 ms admission ramps and 20 ms settling intervals**. The voltage-floor check includes the entire first transition and measures minima of approximately 4.886 V cold and 4.937 V hot in those fixtures; a 5 ms ramp alone with immediate re-admission is insufficient. Require local heavy-load switches/enables to control admission and verify actual COMMS TX/current waveforms; COMMS has a default-off TX switch but no approved shared-rail slew guarantee. IHU must implement the local default-off/hardware rail qualification already allocated. Faster actual COMMS transitions require local capacitance, controlled slew or a further EPS transient correction after measurements; they are not accepted by the ramped fixture.

A 2 ms UV reset requests restart but **does not bypass the LTC4364-1 cooldown**. Using 100 nF, +5.2% capacitance, the cited timer endpoints and 1.1 µA discharge gives approximately 7.6 s for the 63-cycle cooldown estimate, using 1.38 V gate-off and 0.125 V retry endpoints. Use **10 s as a development minimum wait**, keep unavoidable startup load ≤0.5 A, and admit heavy loads only after actual local rail qualification and settling. A fixed delay does not prove the rail is good, and low-VCC timing must still be measured. Do not repeatedly pulse SHDN to bypass cooling. The source controller retains autonomous retry/inhibition behavior.

## MOSFET survival verdict

The [independent extraction](../verification/protection/latch/evidence/survival.json) checks drain/gate voltage and a single device's 150 A pulse-current bound against **total bank current**, without assuming equal sharing. The installed-topology fixtures pass these electrical screens. The unguarded hard input short fails internal VGS. No avalanche-energy credit is taken; an energy number alone cannot establish linear SOA.

The thermal calculation assigns **all parallel-bank power to one device**, uses interval endpoint maximum power, and an elevated four-term Foster sensitivity totaling 1.3 K/W. The historical convolution was a rough approximation; the new response is higher at the visually read single-pulse anchor points. It is not a manufacturer-fitted die/package or local-hotspot model, and the convolution is not coupled back into the electrical MOSFET model as self-heating. The worst new rail rise is approximately **41 K**: about 126 °C from an assumed 85 °C case, or 166 °C from a 125 °C case. This is below the 175 °C absolute junction limit in that model but leaves little margin at the hotter case. Establish a **≤100 °C initial case-temperature development requirement**, a 150 °C engineering junction target and verify both the actual transient response and repeated-fault cooling. An 85 °C electronics screening range does not prove an 85 °C MOSFET case.

**Hot linear SOA remains CONDITIONAL, not passed qualification.** IRLR2908 Fig. 8 is a 25 °C case, single-pulse SOA diagram. Its pulse curves, current/voltage trajectories and transient thermal response were inspected separately. Scaling thermal headroom (0.6 at 85 °C, 0.5 at 100 °C, 0.333 at 125 °C) is an engineering screen, not evidence that all local thermal-instability and sharing mechanisms are bounded. The pointwise/energy/temperature metrics do not supply a guaranteed rectangular-pulse SOA for an arbitrary varying waveform. Obtain manufacturer acceptance for the actual waveform/hot case or measured limited-energy evidence with appropriate margins before declaring MOSFET survival complete. If that evidence fails, select a device with accepted hot linear SOA or shorten the permitted fault interval; do not simply average the three MOSFET currents.

Other explicit acceptance gaps: hot minimum inductance, layout/package parasitics and internal-gate inference, shunt/TVS/pulse-resistor acceptance, actual source energy/impedance and consumer voltage-time limits. Both 60 V / 1 A source and capacitor/reservoir bounds are development fixtures, not measured satellite inputs. No bench test, vacuum thermal qualification or PCB integration ran.

## Required next physical evidence

1. Verify exact assembled components, source permission/RBF placement and rail endpoint mapping. Check receiving-board startup current, capacitance and TX/load slew before a power test.
2. Begin with the documented limited-energy source/reservoir envelope and current-limited load fixtures. Capture protected output, controller timer/FLT, drain current, package-source Kelvin VGS and source/guard nodes for startup, minimum load, single release, ramped/abrupt repeated loads, input/output shorts and recovery. Validate bandwidth/parasitic assumptions; package VGS does not directly measure intrinsic die VGS.
3. At cold/hot conditions and an accepted initial case temperature, verify the voltage ceilings, 5 ms load-ramp requirement, no nuisance latch, true-fault cutoff, early reset/cooldown and recovery with ≤0.5 A startup load. Test one fault at a time with a controlled fault fixture.
4. Confirm hot SOA support, current distribution, case/junction thermal response and repeated-fault cooling. Stop acceptance if the source/consumer envelopes or magnetic/capacitance bounds are exceeded; revise the circuit/fixture instead of relabeling the result.
